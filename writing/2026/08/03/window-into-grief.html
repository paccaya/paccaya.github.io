A window into grief

By Ryan Chan

03 Aug 2026 11:10 AM
dreamy landscape by MonikaMurren, CC BY 2.0

When I look at my life I realise that the mistakes I have made, the things I really regret, were not errors of judgement but failures of feeling. —Jeanette Winterson

For the most part, grief is not a problem to be solved, not a condition to be medicated, but a deep encounter with an essential experience of being human. Grief becomes problematic when the conditions needed to help us work with grief are absent. [...] We are told to "get on with it" and "get over it." The lack of courtesy and compassion surrounding grief is astonishing, reflecting an underlying fear and mistrust of this basic human experience. —Francis Weller

Grief dares us to love once more. —Terry Tempest Williams

Receiving communication from the brother of the one who left me pierces so deep with how the person who broke up with me is only willing to communicate to me indirectly. As I remember how she once said in our relationship she most cherished my communication and how forsaken that premise was now.

With her actions of choosing silence and brevity in any response spoke to how disconnected she was to keeping in contact. To staying. I saw the words of her silence so expressing to me: "you are no longer in my circle of care."

And I wanted to rebel that. I wanted to give her the opportunity to choose otherwise. And I exposed myself to that hurt and received exactly what her silence suggested. With how I so longed to stay friends and realized how even in my commitment to that sentiment, it did not mean she would reciprocate.

And what I was so deeply hurt and in sorrow about was how her brother expressed in his hurt how I was a manipulator. In response to how he recollected how she saw how I was writing about her and wanted me to respect her privacy.

Though I may I be sensitive to how she was in distress, I am still connected to my need of expression in this way. Because there is something so relieving in expressing the grief that has become stuck within me. And after receiving a book from a friend, I explored this practice of not shutting down how I feel and also not letting others trangress the bounds of my care and affection.

As if I did not write about this, I would not honor the relationship I had with her. My love for what has been lost returning as mourning within. That needs to be tended to in a space tender. And the book wrote exactly about this... Francis Weller expressing about the fives "gates of grief".

1. everything we love, we will lose
2. the places that have not known love
3. the sorrows of the world
4. what we expected and did not receive
5. ancestral grief

And what I was so connecting to was the fourth gate: how I can grieve the silent expectation that we were going to stay together. And how I hurt so deeply in connection to how even my expectation of communication had been dispelled facing her restriction in how she only shared a chosen silence with me.

And how loud I heard the message underneath. A message expressing to me how she is facing the undertow of feeling when she so longed to be in relief from those sweeping emotions. And Francis Weller spoke to this in the second gate of grief:

There are the places within us that have been wrapped in shame and banished to the farthest shore of our lives.... these neglected pieces of soul live in utter despair.... The proper response to any loss is grief, but we cannot grieve for something that we feel is outside the circle of worth.

Though I am purely guessing with the dim clarity I actually have within her capacity to feel and have safe spaces to touch parts of herself hurting, I do sense how through the expressions of her brother that she is seeking expression, release, and a honoring of what has been torn.

I was sad to receive her brother's hurt in his judgment of my actions to share my grief publically. As I was touching how he was not only mourning her need of privacy but also how he wanted to support her. Yet also how his judgment expressed to me how in intense emotion he could often judge himself. As I reconnected to my past selves and in their torment who often expressed judgment in self-protection to feeling how hurt I was.

To connect this to the quote, I could see how far out she wanted to place me outside her circle of worth that she restricted the love within her to actually metabolize the loss. To touch the grief within her that was wanting to be noticed.

As Weller noticed himself how there is this psychological moratorium embodied in a "psychological moralism" placing an intense pressure to be improving, feeling good, and rising above our problems.

He wrote:

Happiness has become the new mecca, and anything short of that often leaves us feeling that we have done someting wrong or failed to live up to the acknowledged standard. This forces sorrow, pain, fear, weakness, and vulnerability into the underworld, where they fester and mutate into contorted experessions of themselves, often coated in a mantle of shame. People in my practice routinely apologize for their tears or for feeling sad.

And how not only do I see this in myself with how I use the modern amnesia and anesthesia of turning to screens and places where my attention is captured and my emotions are pacified. And how I see this mirrored in others, burning out the relief of simply attending to an inner world under alarm and wailing for attention, care, and compassion.

So, writing about my grief is this practice of re-orienting to the care I so displaced from myself, often in my sensitivity to her. And how in my longing to communicate with her I realized how I actually so longed to communicate with myself. To reflect on how our relationship had been both comforting and constricting.

Realizing how I have been hurt by this system that does not allow for vulnerability and a curiosity in darkening emotions—often named as 'bad' or 'down' in our addiction to what is 'good' and 'up'. And I am touching something that Ailey Jolie so deeply expressed in her sensitivity to world and system so conveniently insensitive:

[sensitive] bodies aren’t broken. They’re often precisely calibrated to feel what others have learned to numb.

and

When they say you’re too sensitive, what they mean is: your sensitivity makes them uncomfortable with their own numbness.

And how I connect to that is how while I am sensitive to respecting her, I dare to love once more. I dare to love once more even if it means to love her in my greatest capacity is to let her go. Or to write about her even when she does not want me to. And to let her go t allow her to explore the world in the absence of my presence. My presence that once found so much joy seeing her grow.

And I want to recognize how I did fail to feel how she needed spaces of safety, listening, and reflection in my self-protective love to tune inward to the intensity of my feeling rather than stay open and receptive to her needs. And how I did fail to allow her to grow outside of the control and restriction that had so harmed her and those around her. Because I am deeply aware of all the hurt I contributed to her. Yet, I know that I cannot control how she feels and who she is. And I balance that in my gratitude to all that I have learned from her. To how though I want to turn towards responsibility, in tending to the wounds I contributed to, I am turning towards holding reverence.

As I trust she will find the medicine within her and around her all present when deeply touching pain.

I am now reminded of this quote from something someone wrote on substack (people will meet you only as deeply as they meet themselves):

there’s a quiet arrogance in youth that disguises itself as hope, it is the belief that your love can finish work another person has refused to begin — that if you remain kind and patient and understanding, a day will come where you will arrive at the version of them that knows how to love you without hurting you.

and it takes an astonishing amount of heartbreak to realise that no one has ever been loved into becoming someone they were unwilling to become for themselves — because every person you meet has travelled only as far into themselves as they’ve had the courage to go.

Maybe in this relationship, I was that youth. In that foolhardy but earnest conviction that I could somehow be responsible enough in her life to heal her of her wounds. As I am meeting how in this split from her, I could only meet her love as far as she had the lengths of her own self-love.
