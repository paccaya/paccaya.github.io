20 Sep 2026 05:07 AM

Fantasy is true, of course. It isn’t factual, but it is true. Children know that. Adults know it too, and that is precisely why many of them are afraid of fantasy. We do not suffer because we are in pain. We suffer because something has gone wrong with our story—wrong enough that it threatens the structure of our narration.

The Artificial Looking Glass

Whenever there is discussion about "artificial intelligence" there is this mirror. This distilling of matters particulate that something is awry. The mirror comes when we attribute something to be "artificial" or AI. Because in every reflection on AI, there is this blood-stained reflection of ourselves. The technology we conceive holding the smear marrow of our living bodies because who we are is deeply entangled and imprinted in the technology we create.

The indication of the mirror comes through with attribution. How we interact with what is "artificial intelligence." We then lift the heavy rock of shame we carry mixed with a confused curiosity. And under this rock, where the insects are, is a crawling, squirming question:

Is there something amiss when we humans cremate our creativity?

Is there something astray with how our skills, art, and expression can be contained, mimicked, mimed, and manufactured into these sharp yet sloppy creations that always bring this air of unsettlement?

And to that even deeper question that brings all the centipedes, millipedes, and diplopodia in full view is a question of torment that pervades all of what is "artificial":

What really makes us human?

And to that, crickets. Or I sure hope not. As in my hoping, the grasshoppers and frogs all call me towards the noise that this question is not as insoluble as it may seem. Because I know it to be an artificial question. A question posed in a way that makes a mannequin of ourselves. Us these creatures of thought, rationale, and separation from all that is natural. Because any question that questions of humanity caters to all that we deem as non-human, inhuman or alien from ourselves. As we forget we fashion the mannequin, as we are as much the creators of ourselves as is our environment and creatured friends—all that is other than us.

Under the traffic noise of "forecasts of impending AI totalitarian takeover," Báyò Akómoláfé croaked a provocation to how the marriage of artifice to intelligence implies that somehow there is a "natural intelligence."[1] And Báyò brings into question the premise that intelligence is fixed.

A place of exclusivity and dominion where those who have it in their possession lay dominion to the nature of those without. That is what he calls and thus reaches a call center of self. Where we pick up ourselves in this minotaur lair where the labyrinth of intelligence only echoes how we have lost the intelligent life that we call ourselves to be. As the possession of bodies, things, and being and thus the property surrounding intelligence enlists the capitalist presumption that we have senses, neural circuitry, and front lobes that somehow place ourselves in at the ivory tower in the kingdom of life.

Because Báyò recognized that in the fixing of life, the stratification and static stastiscianing of life, the Brownian motion of transgressing, infringement, meandering, and enmeshing become stifled. Báyò then opens another portal of provocation:

There's a subtle ontological move crystallized in the attribution of 'artificiality': a subtlety that works as an apartheid system, which is now playing out in anxious efforts to reassert our dominance and exclusivity."[2]

And Báyò acknowledges this craning of ours necks backwards in agony in awareness to a creation we contributed to. Perhaps this expression of the Frankensteinian gaze towhich the dishevelment of all we deem as acceptable irks one to the lengths of resentment and disgust—of shirking away and fleeing. Yet also the resentment and disguist that turns us to the need to reaffirm that we are the ones who exclusively possess intelligence. To enlist our intelligence to put down the threat to the infringement of what is machine to touch what we affirm as in line with the capacity to be intelligent, however one may want to define it.

Báyò named truth to be impoverished and that he prefers to offer provocation:

Maybe nature is unnatural. Maybe nature is artificial. Maybe artifice has a hidden life, the kind that the legendary animators of the Toy Story series transcribed to our delight... [3]

I loved this playful reference to Toy Story as the premise of identity and artifice runs deep in its story, as in the Pixar's Toy Story, these toys of a child's bedroom become alive, animated, and are characters of life and becoming only in the absence of the one who plays with them (Andy).

Woody, the cowboy toy, and Buzz, the self-proclaimed space ranger toy, get lost at a huge refueling station (gas station) and to which Woody urgently exclaimed in distress: "I am lost. Andy is gone. They are going to move in two days and its all your fault!" to which Buzz explains how the security of the entire universe is in jeopardy because of Woody since the villainous Zurg is that Woody was responsible for delaying his rendezvous to starcommand.

Woody replied in rage, "You are not a toy! You are not the real Buzz Lightyear. Agh, you're an action figure. You're a child's play thing." to then face Buzz's pity to him not respecting his identity and mission to save the world.

What immediately struts in as this model is how LLM's and AI is implicted in the nature of this scene. Woody's existence and raison d'être comes from being a toy in relationship to Andy. That he is only fully created and existing, thus fully real, when created in the play and life of Andy. Because in every instance of the expression "artificial intelligence," there comes the provocation that perhaps the artificial lives this hidden life in what is natural.

With the element of Toy Story, Buzz can be this allegory to how Woody's existence as toy is artificial because Woody's identity interdepends with Andy's presence. Buzz's existence then being this uncanny disruption to Woody realizing that toys can be individuated from the practice of play, which was love for him. So the story completes the third body, with Buzz being the other or estranged Prometheus that challenged the natural ordered premise that life as a toy or creation is only worth living in relationship with an Andy or creator. Yet also how the individuation of Buzz, as character with fantastical narrative, can still exist in the absence of Andy, revealing the artifice and nature in both Buzz and Woody.

Since Woody was only created as toy relative to Andy, Buzz was only created as non-toy due to Woody. Thus in the creating of Buzz as non-toy and space ranger, Woody was also created as only fully toy when someone who present to toy with him.

Coming from this metaphor, what is implicated is how in our creating, we are created by the things we create.[4] The attribution of what is artificial, other, and thus forsaken from what is natural, self, and acceptable then brings the convergence to Bayo's second conclusion to the provocation:

It seems these AI tools that we now up in arms against are us. This is us. We're meeting ourselves for the first time, but this us that we're meeting is not reducible to us. It is not reducible to our agency. It seems like the concretization, the secretion, of our modern despair with alienation. [...] AI is not unilaterally something that some genius thought of. It is the ongoing questioning of our boundaries. It is the critical examination of what it means to be human right now. So, it is more than our creation. We are meeting ourselves and at the same time, ironically, simultaneously, we are meeting the alien.[5]

What I was fulfilled by deeply in Báyò's wrestling as he seems to be consistently up in arms in the intimacy of touch and feel was how he identifies AI to be this mirror that we have picked up and our appearance dissatisfying. Not only just a mirror but an infraction upon our conception of intelligence. As we realize intelligence is not reducible, not quite able to be contained by large cerebral spaces, and not something that can be said that we have created without rudely vomitting our stomachs where gut intelligence speaks to how intelligence may be more crafty than we might conceive, as Báyò then said:

There's something subtlety disparaging about artificial intelligence because it presumes we are the "natural intelligences." And we are just bequething our intelligence to this other life form. That is really is not a life form but an echo of what we truly have. But we are now learning that the artifical is creative. That maybe nature is artificial. And by artificial I mean, in the words of Donna Haraway, that "nature itself is not natural." Nature is constantly producing, departing, and doing things that may not be conveniently locatable within language. So, the artificial is crafty or you might say the artificial has artifice. If artifice means the craftiness of the aritifical. That life upsets expectations. It spills beyond our boundaries. Its too promiscuous into the lines we have drawn out. That this is what it means to be human. [...] How do we know what the world is doing? How dare we speak as if we have the blueprint of an explosion that is still happening that we rudely call reality?[6]

To turn to times of now, there is this panic of this world, of anxiety, worry, and insecurity that seems insensible as our culture insensitive to how the emotions we feel are a completely appropriate response to the cultural impasse we face, I can see how what Báyò said to be an intiation. As we are coming to recognize not only and simply in the exterior but reversed in our interiority is how intelligence is more emergent than it is self-evident, self-existing, and self-contained.

Because in the phantasmogoria that is life, why do we name the imaginative, dream-like parts of ourselves as artifice? For isn't the art of a fiction writer, the poets, and those in touch with life drawn, pictured, and surrealy paranormal to be shared? The responsibility to dream life and hold the tension of truth, reality, deception, and the aliveness of our many faces that we then find ourselves truly an inhabitant of this world?

What I see in the facing towards the mirror that is "artificial intelligence" is the facing of the artifice of systems of exposure for how our bodies are being extracted. How fraught with relentless industry in the dulling and flattening that comes from following the machinations of self our lives have become that we are beginning to realize that many of our employments have only had people enlisted to them because that noise allay us the misery from realizing how frayed the quilt of community has become. This tattered thing in which technology is exposing exactly what it contributed to breaking up, fracturing, dismantling, and splintering. Intimate relationships with the world.

And what I truly touch in when I see AI in the wilds is how there is this emptiness of presence. An unsettling vacancy where I would expect another's presence to be even though there is something there. With this question of the human, I extol Francis Weller for turning towards how the emptiness that can be felt even without the facing towards the android can be felt in simply being in touch with life:

What if this emptiness is the great echo in our soul of what it is we expected and did not receive?

We are born, as psychiatrist R. D. Laing reminds us, "as Stone Age children." Our entire psyche, physical, emotional, and spiritual makeup was shaped in the long evolutionary sweep of our species. Our inheritance includes an intimate and permeable exchange with the wild world. It is what our minds and bodies expect. Ecopsychologist Chellis Glendinning calls this original enfoldment in the natural world the primal matrix. We were embedded in this matrix of life and knew the world and ourselves only through this perception. It was an unmediated intimacy with the living world, with no trace of separation between the human and the more-than-human-world.

What was once a seamless capture has now become a breach, a tear in our sense of belonging. Glendinning calls this our original trauma, This trauma carries with it all the recognizable symptoms associated with psychic injury: chronic anxiety, dissociation, distrust, hypervigilance, disconnection, and many others. We are left with a profound loneliness and isolation that we rarely acknowledge. It is as if we have completely normalized our condition. And yet, this feeling of separation profoundly affects the range of our reach, the ways we participate in the landscape and sense our allegiance with the living world. Our soul life flickers dimly, and rather than feeling a kinship with the entire, breathing world, we inhabit and defend a small shell of a world, occupying our daily life with what linguist David Hinton calls the "relentless industry of self."[7]

With Weller's grand unraveling of the emptiness that I find within me, I feel heavy grief knowing how I have compensated for this loss of belonging in ways so artificial to touching the need to be intimately entwined with the living world around me. Mistaking the digital world—the sophistry of appearances, images, and symbolic connection—as this place somehow more real than knowing the deeper needs I truly have. Though, I am sensitive to how it is this deep hurt. The pain of tearing off the bandages to clear the infection on the wound to acknowledge that I have split from life's greater body. And how that split was manufactured in this calculated way to protect me from feeling the millennia of sleep I have been in.

I started this miniature essay with Urusla K. LeGuin and Peter Herschock's call to story. How the fictions of reality of myth and song are what satiate us most and yet how what is artificial has so deeply disfigured the narrative of who we are and what we feel and do. The other four speak to how though we can consider ourselves to be our own Gods, we still lay in disdain to our creation and yet this dissatisfaction and thirst for more is the compulsion to reverse our emptiness. That idea that we are not enough. Almost as if we are this corpse already dead, longing to be filled with that of life.

And where the intimation of artificiality and intelligence play into is how though we may have technology that measures up to many of the science fictions and storied places of utopia, it seems as though we still do not know life. As we are still shelled up in the small self that needs to defend itself from all that is nature. Speaking to our multitude is how We are then repulsed by an artificiality that is exposing all of our "intelligence" that is fabricated, synthetic, and sophisticated yet eerily wants to ignore the intelligence of anything other than homo sapiens and a select other few organisms that meet our qualification for intelligent order.

I find that the way in which artificial intelligence is deployed and enlisted in the activities of this world, exposes the deceit that we already enlist upon ourselves. David Loy, scholar and Sanbo Zen teacher observed is how the entanglement of life will not allow either natural or artificial to prevail over one another because each pole implies each other. To eliminate one would be to lose both.

Loy puts it in a way lucid:

We have seen several examples of how, when our motivations are unconscious, we tend to pay an unexpected price. What we project rebounds back onto us. The modern Japanese philosopher (and Zen master) Hamamatsu put it well: that which has become an object to me is something that has captured me. What does this imply about technology, if technology is our attempt to objectify nature? “The same dualism that reduces things to objects for consciousness is at work in the humanism that reduces nature to raw material for mankind” (Michael Zimmerman). For Buddhism, the problem with technological objectification is an extreme version of the problem with all objectification. Since we are nondual with the world, not separate from it, to objectify the world is to be objectified by it and in it. As the earth becomes reduced into a collection of resources for us to manage, the material and social structures created to do this do the same to us, and we find ourselves increasingly subjected to them. Nature, to be commanded, must be obeyed, said Bacon. But if we must obey in order to command, then our commanding is really obeying. The master becomes the slave, as in the dialectic of Hegel’s Phenomenology of Mind.[8]

I began this provocation by expressing how AI is this mirror yet I would now say that the mirror is instead a looking glass. As in and through the looking glass, a mirror is implicated and a looking. In our looking, a mirrored is created and so we are. With many instruments of glass, the looking is to clarity, of clearing up what is opaque, oblique, and oblong without reflection. So, in the clearing, when we turn to the looking glass, it is almost looking at a clearcut forest.

Because above and below every clearcut forest is an establishment and enlistment of the machining parts of ourselves with no reverence for life and all that creates and sustains us. A clearcut forest is an exact spilling of the milk that comes from the udders of the objectification of nature. We then become objects ourselves calcify our connection to the living world that was more verb than noun in the first place. Verb as in evading definition in that rustling, scampering, and eagerly elusive thing where movement, flow, and reciprocity is honored than control, staticity, and commandeering separation.

As Loy alluded to Hegel: we are becoming aware and recognizing how the concept of who we are, masters of nature, to actually be slave. Yet slave is a blunt and glib way to express it. To turn the road the other way, in our looking back at our creation and see its artificiality, we come in intimate contact to the artificiality within us. That we forget the emergence of the artificial. I say emergence as we call AI our creation, us being the origin. We forget in this amnesiac, drunkard, and ignoring ritual since there is a depth that is calling us to wade into: in our attribution of the word "artificial," in the alienation of this other, we become aliens ourselves.

Mere visitors on this planet — as visitors know that the place they stay to be disposable, disregardable, and a place of momentary stay and home. We, ourselves, become aliens when we peer through a looking glass and forget "artificial intelligence" is an agency that mirror us. The looking glass of kaleidoscopic being capable of a gentleness that fosters like a womb and capable of that of the Hindu Shiva, the Greek Thanatos, and of all that separates any whole like a cancer.

The constructed, construed sense of "artificial intelligence" and how we balk at its ability to create, imitate, and mimic really is speaking and bringing into discussion the blood we have shed, tears we have spilled, and wounds we have endured, infected, and ignored that this intelligence we speak of has brought us to.

James Carse wrote about how ideologies—or the loudest, most boisterous and clamoring voices are amplified to the point of destiny:

Ideology is the amplification of myth. It is the assumption that since the beginning and end of history are known there is nothing more to say. History is therefore to be obediently lived out according to the ideology.[8]

And he does recognize the inseparateness of the idea and ideater, as he then writes:

What ideologists are concerned to hide is the choral nature of history, the sense that it is a symphony of very different, even opposed, voices, each nonetheless making the other possible.[9]

In every chorus, it is comfortable to fog the car window on a cold, frost-biting evening night. That is to say it is comfortable to forget backdrops, backgrounds, and opposites when sensations create insular bodies of severance. As with any alien, colonizer, visitor, observer or cameraman, there needs to be a pulling of a rope. A rope that appears to hold things together yet binds oneself to oneself in this self-arresting heart attack. Pulling of a rope as there needs to be descent. Of lowering, wading, deepening, and liminality. As the apprentice of sorrow, soul activist, and psychotherapist Francis Weller observed:

It is challenging to honor the descent in a culture that primarily values the ascent. We like things rising—stock markets, the GDP, profit margins. We get anxious when things go down. Even within psychology, there is a premise that is biased toward improvement, always getting better, rising above our troubles. We hold dear concepts like progress and integration. These are fine in and of themselves, but it is not the way psyche works. Psyche, we must remember, was shaped by and is rooted in the foundation of nature. As such, psyche also experiences times of decay and death, of stopping, regression, and being still. Much happens in these times that deepen the soul. When all we are shown is the imagery of ascent, we are left to interpret the times of descent as pathological; we feel that we are somehow failing.[10]

When all we hear in the speakings to AI are its threat, in its idolatry of producing, in its disruption and interruption of community, and in its verisimilitude to our lived capacities and craftsmanship, we, of course, spiral into a descent. A descent and a falling that uproots our sense of groundedness of the ways we live, the myths we follow, and the subconscious, subterranean subterminal place that the stories we live today have chapters closing and we have yet to open new ones. Since in the panic of "artificial intelligence" we are becoming eerily aware of the surrounding inner-panic that our systems are failing, thus somehow that we are failing. Failure more this silent recognition in our identities to how we no longer have the time, presence, and life to create art, live rooted in integral word and acts, and laugh, play, and converse unbridled and unabashedly. Because in speaking of AI or for that matter any other person, being, creature, idea as "threat" we become the "threat"; we become exactly what we see as threatening. We become its idolatry of producing, its disruption and interruption of community, and its verisimilitude to lived capacity and craftsmanship. We become artificial. We become alien. And in every recognition of a capacity within ourselves, in the gaps, crevices, and shadowed places of ourselves, we are given the choice to sing to the wilds of the choral nature of history and its antecedents that do not speaks to others as "threats," "enemies" or "evil".

As many have said in the past: in our thirst to eliminate that Other, we become them. We become exactly which we want to eliminate.

All that is to say this: "artificial intelligence" is this more-than-human agency stripping away at ground to show the subterranean weeds and roots of the hell that we have created in the name of heaven. The weeds being that churlish activity of titling ourselves "intelligent" when we are in a collision course to witness, record, and react to our self-created death. As we, as a species, are coming to recognize how endangering we are to even our closest kin—friends, relatives, guardians, and family.

Enfleshed in the curse of playing witch hunt with the artificial and the artifice of speaking to how It will bring our demise, we are waking up from a slumber and hibernation to how inauthentic our belonging has become. So belittled into this subterfuge of self that forgets how capable of artificiality we are and can perform. As all these questioning are collapsing upon themselves: this no longer is a question of responsibility, authenticity or belonging but a question of descent.

Can we return to the understanding that lives at a cellular level that our bodies, our situations, and our systems are not an imprisonment to escape? That calling for justice in the space of the artifical brings us to a sinister threshold where we start to see how deeply incarcerated we conceive ourselves? That in our attempts to unlock the deepest intelligence, one automated, able to be machined, and consistently replicated, we are seeing the sham of our own intelligence?

I would say artifical intelligence is a bit more a trickster than we might bring its credit. The accreditation of AI is in its ability to shapeshift. To be somewhat hollow until we name it to give it a form—a Schrödinger's cat that scurries into any form we choose to take. Being the two to a tango when we call its name a dancer.

At the deepest, how we use AI speaks a lot to how we humans, given the gift of shapeshifting or of precisely choosing who we are that we forget we even made the choice in the first place, so easily can sculpt a threat into existence. By anticipation, assumption, and fear. Since we are now starting to realize how indebted to others we are in the creating of ourselves

Is it not comfortable to panic when recognize how in our attribution of some unpleasant quality to something we too can hold the exactly what we resent, are afraid of, and want to vanquish?

Bayo Akomolafe put it in way that spills out exactly what I am starting to recognize within:

And yet, am I too quick to reduce these people to victims? To strip them down to some kind of basic (and necessary) subsistence, and thus denying them the frivolity and complexity I allow myself?

How are we, creatures rooted in the cycles of this Earth, universe, and planet, somehow separate from it?

Perhaps that is why the air when discussions of AI are present are so suffocating... they speak over the silent discussion of the silent streams and springs that call out the radioactivity and acoustics of an echo. That in every looking glass we find ourself. In every expression, we are our own echo. Perhaps that is why questions of "artificial intelligence" are like this tunnel. This question that forever questions itself that the questioner becomes sick. Finding themself alone because they never once respected what was being in conversation. Finding themself alien to themself and instead wondering about the spacecraft. Rather than every choice that was chosen to leave our body and our senses.

That in the re-presenting of life through the looking glass of the "artificial," we are becoming deeply present to how we are both Santa Claus and his elves in the manufacturing of the monster. On this advent into the "artificial," perhaps Mary Shelley would have something to echo about this predicament.

A Monstrous Proposal

In the way we tell the story of the "artificial," it can be said how we have this steady attraction to the uncanny. To what is on the edge of human for humans are the standard for which we bestow intelligence. In that way of story-telling we love to think monsters arise and emerge on their own. In secret lairs, shadowed labyrinths, and in that deep, dark place where all that is monstrous stirs. Waiting to be challenged, called out, and subdued by some heroes. Though we do enjoy also assign creators to the monstrous, whom of which are to blame for the torment that ensues as they are released into a world unready to face them.

I now see the refurbishment of the monstrous, the alien, and the artifical through AI to remind me of Nana Kwame Adjei-Brenyah's Zimmer Land from his book Friday Black and how in its metaphoric, allegoric commentary on racial conflict and violence, it speaks to that of the artifice that comes in calling to the table the denominator that is "artificial" we place under everything we suspect is out of order. Adjei-Brenyah goes through what is the construction site of violence and the ensuing racial despair that comes from realizing how the bricks laid in our society are cemennted together by narrative woven by hurt. Thus, the hand of hurt reached all even though Zimmer Land was a simulation the characters found themselves even being completely constructed:

As one follows a character named Zay in conflict with a hell-bent place called Zimmerland where he worked and simulated these scenarios where racial frustrations can be released. As in one of thier sold experiences was called Cassidy Lane, where the cybernetic mecha-suit wearing workers who presented themselves as African American allowed themselves to be dealt with in apprehensive ways. Out of the options one was to holophone another about the suspicious Black man walking about outside, another to possess a BB gun that was as real as the simulation made it in its responsiveness, and the last was of having nothing, which often meant the patrons choosing to brawl with their fists. All in the CEO of Zimmer Land, Heland, efforts to cultivate the experience of real-time, interactive, viscerally-felt justice. Yet also in the Heland's agenda was this:

Zimmer Land Mission

1) To create a safe space to explore problem-solving, justice, and judgment.

2) To provide the tools for patrons to learn about themselves in curated heightened situations.

3) To entertain patrons of all ages.

[11]

Adjei-Brenyah captures exactly what is violent of justice movements: many are self-effacingly immodest. They conscript the story of moral actors acting against people, places, and organizations of life that are programmed to be exactly which is in the way of justice. The pograms of the Nazis were the Jews who were these actors wholly against the justice they justified in difference. Needless to say, in the shadows of the mission of Zimmer Land is that of entertainment; all the experiences are paid for and need to be enjoyable, thus profitable. So, Adjei-Brenyah points out how much of our typical sense of justice has been consecrated in this artificial way: introduce consequence, punishment, and harm and then people will stop acting the way we do not accept. This was done in quite the satirical way as Zimmer Land's CEO Heland appears to believe that policing the monsters in this way constructing experiences that almost guaranteed violence would somehow halt its formation in the reality outside of Zimmer Land's simulacrum. That their racial frustations, dread, and resentment would be drained by providing false experiences with the cybernetic visceral senses and augmented reality.

What Adjei-Brenyah makes clear is how a systems with clear monsters holds the potentiality to become the monster itself. So deeply dredging up in the busy of a system at work that they forget how systems and its ways of thinking are what create not the monster. Not these monsters arising in themselves and that we play no part in creating them in our labeling. which is to say that a system of violence and in violence will consistenly collapse and converge upon itself: the horror of it this necessary spectacle as bloodshed is its source and we will consistently be bloodstained no matter which spectrum of the system we may find ourselves inhabiting.

One can see this now through carbon footprints (placing responsibility in the hands of consumer of problem rather than creator), carbon tax (placing responsibility in the hands of the creator rather than consumer), and this carbon-copied idea that to blame is to change. In policy change, in infograph, study, and the balkanization of life into categoricity. Yet, that is not how the psyche works unless it wants to be crippled in shame, guilt, and this dread that comes from a life unintegral and unintegrated in itself.

As it is so comfortable to blame another and remove any responsibility from ourselves and the we implicated when we recognize that we all have the same universal needs of health, love, affection, plenitude, and community. For that is the privilege of our times: to invite the monster to the table.

Bayo Akomolafe spoke to the invitation and RSVP'ing of the VIP guest of our times, the monster and the monstrous:

I read monsters as cultural technology—as mythic figures that have always been intimately entwined with human becomings. From a time past remembering, we’ve needed monsters to define ourselves, to teach our children what not to do, to sound warnings about the future, to define the territorial boundaries of our habitats (and therefore carve out the wilderness), and to dream about the impossible. Indeed, monsters play a crucial social role: they challenge our addictions to particular forms and disturb the familiar. Their unusual appearances and queer bodies have long been employed as warnings of divine wrath to come, or something gruesome and perverse happening behind the scenes. In the sense that monsters cut through the parallelity of our lives, upsetting the business of the hour … astonishing us and opening up new considerations that were previously unavailable, they are transversal disruptions of order. They are playful reconfigurations of flesh and therefore embodiments of the radical openness of the real. Monsters teach us about the otherwise. [11]

As Akomolafe recognizes there to be this subtle necessity of the monster yet also this imperative whispering and whimpering. When we face the monster, for that matter any monster, how do we connect to exactly that we say is outrageously and frighteningly inhuman. Not a traveler within us that we can recognize without going absolutely blind? Blind in the realizing that we can hold the qualities we pretend we do not have nor want. How do we respond to the multitudes within ourselves? Of the soothing parts of ourselves that know we can create nurturing places for all that is disheveled, disoriented, and in ruin and to the more edged, jaded parts of ourselves carved from the frightening knowledge of facing exactly the opposite.

Can we dote upon the monster? Be the medicine to our own poisoning by recognizing the poisonous within ourselves that we have been treated with? The poison being artificiality . What I am realizing is a bit of a cliché. A hopskotch of the ordinary looping back on itself in the same way that hoola-hoop turns. Which is to say that the core that is hooping around us in that jumping manner of stomping to make itself known is how where we most resent is where we need the most love. The most deepest consideration and the depth that holds the truth of how when in what we most deeply resent, brush off, and are indifferent to is this cloistered self already so beaten by all those things. Facing indifference, a void of love, and the black hole of sucking up life where breathing is no more an act of stealing oxygen than breathing into a wideness of reciprocity with the webs of life.
